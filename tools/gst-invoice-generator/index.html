Everything You Need to Know About GST Invoicing in India

Under India's Goods and Services Tax framework governed by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC), every registered business that supplies taxable goods or services must issue a valid tax invoice. An invoice is not merely a payment request; it is a legal document that enables your clients to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC) on their monthly GSTR-3B filings.

Mandatory Fields on a Valid GST Tax Invoice

To avoid penalties and ensure that your customers can claim ITC seamlessly, every tax invoice must contain the following primary details:

  • Supplier Identity: Name, registered business address, and 15-digit GSTIN of the supplier.
  • Invoice Number: A consecutive, unique serial number containing only alphabets, numerals, and special characters (hyphen and slash), not exceeding 16 characters.
  • Date of Issue: The date on which the invoice is generated and provided to the recipient.
  • Recipient Identity: Name and address of the recipient, along with their GSTIN if they are registered.
  • HSN / SAC Code: Harmonized System of Nomenclature code for physical goods or Services Accounting Code for services.
  • Place of Supply: The name of the destination state along with its two-digit GST state code.
  • Tax Rate Breakdown: Individual values for CGST, SGST, or IGST applied to each line item.

Intra-State vs Inter-State GST: Understanding the Difference

The rules of GST taxation depend entirely on the geographic relationship between the location of the supplier and the Place of Supply:

1. Intra-State Supply (Same State): When the supplier and the customer are located within the same state (for example, a software vendor in Bihar supplying software services to a retailer in Patna, Bihar), the tax is split equally into Central GST (CGST) and State GST (SGST). If the product carries an 18% GST slab, 9% is allocated to CGST and 9% to SGST.

2. Inter-State Supply (Different States): When the supplier is in one state and the recipient is in another (for example, a software agency in Bihar billing an enterprise client in Delhi or Mumbai), the transaction attracts Integrated GST (IGST). The full 18% is billed directly as IGST, which the Central Government collects and apportions to the destination consumer state.
